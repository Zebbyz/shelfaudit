Woolworths NZ: no ComCom fine yet. Criminal charges over till prices and fake specials (Jan 2022 to Sep 2024) are still before the court and Woolworths has pleaded not guilty. FY26 (year to June 2026) sales $8.491b, up 2.5% from $8.286b; EBIT $163m, up 8.8% from $150m. The current maximum fine per charge is $600,000, about 0.4% of one year's EBIT.

Foodstuffs North Island: FY24 (year to 31 March 2024) revenue $9.235b, up 9.1%; EBIT $292m; member store revenue $10.8b. We could not find published FY25 or FY26 accounts, so FY24 is the latest used. Pak'nSave stores are separately owned member businesses, so group figures show scale, not each store's own books.

If Pak'nSave is the best value in NZ, why are its stores being convicted?

Pak'nSave sells itself as New Zealand's lowest prices. Yet Pak'nSave stores have been convicted for charging shoppers more at the till than the shelf said: Mangere in 2020, then Silverdale and Mill Street in 2026 across 18 charges. A cheap shelf ticket means nothing if the till charges more. ComCom says the Silverdale and Mill Street errors were repeated, after years of warnings to the major supermarkets.

Surely the answer is they all are, and these two are just the only ones caught. OR the others make so much money they don't need to? We doubt that very much.

With fines this small next to the money flowing through the co-op, a pricing error costs less than fixing the system. ComCom itself says a civil regime with fines up to $5 million a breach is needed for real deterrence. Check your receipt.
