The duopoly, in the regulator's own words

20 Oct 2026 · Shelf Audit

Duopoly Commerce Commission

In 2022 the Commerce Commission found competition between Woolworths and Foodstuffs was muted and their profits were well above normal.

This is not a conspiracy theory. It is the finding of a 609-page government market study.

What the Commerce Commission found in March 2022

  • Woolworths NZ and Foodstuffs "operated effectively as a duopoly" in a $22 billion a year industry.[1][2]
  • Competition between them was "muted".[2]
  • Average returns on capital from 2015 to 2019 were 12.7% (Woolworths), 12.8% (Foodstuffs South Island) and 13.1% (Foodstuffs North Island). A normal return was estimated at 5.5%.[1]
  • NZ grocery prices "appear relatively high by international standards".[1]

"About $1 million a day"

The then Commerce Minister, David Clark, described the sector as making excess profits of around $1 million a day.[3] That was the Government's summary of the excess return, not a daily accounting profit.

Four years on

The Commission's 2025 Annual Grocery Report, published 2 June 2026, says the major retailers still hold 82% of the market, an HHI of 3,585 shows "limited competitive pressure", and both Foodstuffs co-ops "sit at the top end of international profitability benchmarks".[4]
