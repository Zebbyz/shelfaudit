Your unpaid second job at the checkout

22 Oct 2026 · Shelf Audit

Self-checkout Staff

You scan it, bag it and fix the unexpected item. Consumer NZ says the staff savings are not being passed on to shoppers.

You scan it. You bag it. You fix the "unexpected item in the bagging area". You wait in line to do it. None of that comes off your bill.

How big self-checkout has become

In 2023, around one in four Foodstuffs South Island purchases went through self-service, and over half at one Christchurch New World, 1News reported.[2]

Who keeps the saving

Supermarkets save on staff, and "those savings are not currently being passed on to shoppers."

That is Consumer NZ, speaking to RNZ.[1]

What shoppers and staff say

  • 66% of Kiwis prefer a cashier over self-service, a Canstar Blue survey found in 2019.[3]
  • In the 2026 Workers First retail staff survey, workers linked understaffing to more safety incidents, and more staff was the top fix they asked for.[4]

Overseas, some are going back

UK chain Booths removed self-checkouts from all but two stores from November 2023, and later reported less theft and faster service.[5] A 2024 Drexel University study found shoppers who use self-checkout for bigger baskets (more than 15 items) are less loyal to the store.[6]

Our view

Opinion, Guy Hollister, Shelf Audit: If you are doing the work, you should share the saving. Cramped bagging areas and long self-serve queues are a choice the store makes, and the shopper pays for it in time.
