How suppliers are treated, and the code meant to fix it

26 Oct 2026 · Shelf Audit

Suppliers Grocery Supply Code Commerce Commission

Rebates and payments from suppliers hit about $6 billion in FY25. Both big groups have had Grocery Supply Code warnings.

  • In 2022 the Commission found the major groups use buying power to shift costs and risks onto suppliers.[1]
  • Rebates, discounts and payments between the major retailers and suppliers were worth about $6 billion in FY25, 26% of their combined sales revenue.[2]
  • In a 2024 survey of 562 suppliers, 35% rated their experience with Foodstuffs North Island negative or very negative, against 19% for Woolworths and 17% for Foodstuffs South Island.[3]
  • In July 2025 the Commission warned Foodstuffs North Island it had likely breached the Grocery Supply Code by obstructing and delaying a supplier's request.[4]
  • In December 2025 it warned Woolworths for likely failing to tell suppliers of their right to a review of delisting decisions.[5]

A warning is the Commission's view of a likely breach, not a court finding.
