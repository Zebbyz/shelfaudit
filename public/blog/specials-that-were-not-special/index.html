Specials that were not special: the court cases

30 Oct 2026 · Shelf Audit

Pricing Commerce Commission Court

Two Pak'nSaves have been convicted and fined $580,500. Woolworths faces criminal charges it has pleaded not guilty to.

On 30 October 2026, Pak'nSave Silverdale and Pak'nSave Mill Street were fined a combined $580,500 in the Auckland District Court after pleading guilty to inaccurate pricing under the Fair Trading Act.[1]

  • Silverdale: $234,000 for 10 charges. Mill Street: $346,500 for 8 charges.[1]
  • The charges included Pams Value white bread advertised at $1.15 but charged at $1.19, tissues shown at $2 but charged at $3, and Vogel's bread promoted as "extra low" when it was dearer than at any point in the past 2 years.[2]

Woolworths

The Commission filed criminal charges against Woolworths NZ in May 2025. It alleges that between January 2022 and September 2024, advertised prices did not match checkout prices, and some specials were not genuine.[3] Woolworths has pleaded not guilty while it seeks more detail, and says it has 3.5 million transactions a week and refunds overcharges in full.[4] These are allegations, not findings.

Fines that fit

The maximum fine today is $600,000 per charge. The Commission says proposed law changes would allow up to $5 million per breach.[5] Consumer NZ research found 62% of supermarket shoppers noticed pricing errors in the past year.[2]

Opinion: Check your receipt. Every time.
